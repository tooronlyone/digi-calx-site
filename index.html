01 Why explore this

The opportunity

Calibration coordination can be easier to understand.

Finding a laboratory is only one part of the task. A factory also needs to understand whether the lab fits the instrument, measurement range, service location and any stated technical requirement.

Digi_CalX is being explored as a structured layer around that process—not as a replacement for competent laboratory work, technical review or accreditation bodies.

01

Scattered discovery

Suitable capability can be difficult to compare across calls, messages and separate documents.

02

More than a category

A broad label such as “pressure calibration” may not confirm range, method, location or scope fit.

03

Disconnected records

Quotes, custody updates, decisions, evidence and certificates may sit in different channels.

02 The product concept

Three connected views

One proposed system.
Different responsibilities.

The direction combines a factory-facing discovery experience, a laboratory workflow and an evidence trail. These are planned product layers, not currently available services.

FACTORY VIEW01

Ask clearly.

Capture an instrument and service need through guided inputs, then compare laboratories that pass relevant eligibility checks.

  • Instrument registry
  • Guided service request
  • Comparable quotes
LAB VIEW02

Work visibly.

Organize capabilities, job intake, custody, technical records, review and certificate delivery within a connected workflow.

  • Capability profile
  • Job progression
  • Review trail
EVIDENCE VIEW03

Trust carefully.

Keep important job events and certificate versions linked so decisions can be understood without reducing trust to a rating.

  • Custody events
  • Versioned records
  • Structured disputes
03 Proposed workflow

Request → record

A service journey designed to stay connected.

This illustrates the intended workflow. Exact screens, rules and commercial steps remain subject to validation.

01
DEFINE

Register the instrument

Add identity and available technical details without requiring the requester to master every metrology term.

02
REQUEST

Describe the service need

State service location, timing, known measurement points and certificate or scope requirements.

03
FILTER

Find technically eligible labs

Check relevant capability, range, location and stated requirements before considering price or popularity.

04
SELECT

Compare clear quotes

Review proposed service, logistics, turnaround and verification signals on a like-for-like basis.

05
TRACK

Follow the physical job

Connect handover or on-site check-in, condition notes, job events and relevant authorizations.

06
PRESERVE

Receive the reviewed record

Keep the approved certificate, revision history, return status and future due information together.

04 Matching principle

Eligibility before ranking

Suitable first.
Popular second.

Price, speed and customer feedback only become useful comparisons after a laboratory appears technically suitable for the stated request.

!Paid visibility must never turn a technically ineligible laboratory into an eligible match.

05 Clear boundaries

What the concept does not claim

Software can support trust.
It cannot manufacture it.

LAB

The laboratory calibrates

Digi_CalX is intended to organize the digital journey. Physical measurement remains the responsibility of competent laboratory personnel.

ACC

Accreditation stays with the lab

The platform may display checked scope evidence. Using the platform would not make a laboratory accredited.

HUM

Technical approval stays human

Automation may assist future workflows, but results, conformity decisions and certificate approval remain technical responsibilities.

REC

Original evidence should remain

Adjustments should not erase as-found observations, and certificate corrections should create traceable revisions.
